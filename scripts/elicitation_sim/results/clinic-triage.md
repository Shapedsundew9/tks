**[t1] Dr. Sarah (stakeholder):** We operate two rural walk-in clinics with rotating nurses and visiting physicians, and we need a consistent way to handle patient triage and urgent care handoffs during peak hours.

**[t2] Facilitator (facilitator):** [A2: Primary Stakeholders & Agency] If a patient arrives during peak hours and both the on-duty nurse and visiting physician are unavailable, does the triage protocol assign responsibility to the clinic administrator or the next-arrival physician?

**[t3] Dr. Sarah (stakeholder):** Neither. Clinic administrators only handle scheduling and transport, while triage nurses perform initial severity classification; if a visiting physician is delayed, the local nurse practitioner holds emergency stabilization authority.

**[t4] Facilitator (facilitator):** [Evidence & Accountability] If a nurse practitioner stabilizes a patient during a physician's delay, is the decision to escalate care required to be documented in the patient's chart for audit purposes?

**[t5] Dr. Sarah (stakeholder):** Our rules do not specify audit charting for care escalation. We only require contemporaneous dual-signoff if an acuity override occurs.

**[t6] Facilitator (facilitator):** [Temporal Cadence & Lifecycle] If a patient arrives during peak hours and the assigned physician isn't available within 15 minutes, does the triage protocol automatically escalate to a remote specialist or wait until the physician arrives?
