**[t1] Elena (stakeholder):** We manage regional refrigerated freight transport for perishable food suppliers, and we have recurring disputes over temperature excursions and spoilage liability at handover docks.

**[t2] Facilitator (facilitator):** [A1: Core Operational Problem & Desired Outcome] Is the core operational problem the occurrence of temperature excursions leading to spoilage, or the disputes over liability for such excursions?

**[t3] Elena (stakeholder):** Both matter: receiving managers must reject cargo whenever temperature excursions exceed 45 continuous minutes, while logged telemetry and physical seals must be countersigned by both driver and manager to resolve release of liability.

**[t4] Facilitator (facilitator):** [Temporal Cadence & Lifecycle] If a temperature excursion occurs during transit but the handover is delayed beyond the scheduled window, does the liability for spoilage shift to the receiving party regardless of the excursion duration, or is the liability determined solely by the excursion's duration during transit?

**[t5] Elena (stakeholder):** Liability does not shift due to handover delays; release of liability strictly requires countersigned telemetry data and seal serial numbers. If an excursion exceeds 45 continuous minutes, the receiving manager must reject the cargo within 30 minutes of arrival.

**[t6] Facilitator (facilitator):** [Exception & Conflict Resolution] If a dispute arises over the validity of telemetry data due to a missing seal, does the receiving manager have the authority to override the driver's countersignature and declare the cargo as non-releasable, or must the dispute be escalated to a third-party arbitrator for resolution?
